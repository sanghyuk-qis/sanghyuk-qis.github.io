About

My research path connects precision measurement with programmable quantum systems: from hot vapor to single cold atom for quantum science.

Background

I received my B.S. in Physics from Korea University, where coursework and research exposure in atomic physics motivated my interest in AMO systems and precision measurement.

Previous focus

At the Agency for Defense Development, I worked on optically pumped magnetometers, Rb–Xe nuclear magnetic resonance gyroscopes, vapor-cell fabrication, and spin-relaxation analysis.

Current direction

I am joining Seoul National University to help construct an Rb–Yb mixed-species neutral-atom tweezer array platform for quantum information science.

Research

I organize my work into two connected themes: earlier work on atomic sensors and current work on neutral-atom platforms for quantum information science.

Old: OPMs and NMR gyroscopes

My previous research focused on alkali–noble-gas vapor-cell systems for precision sensing. This included mode-hop assisted all-optical atomic magnetometry, heading-error analysis, compact Rb–Xe atom spin gyroscopes, optical-contact-bonded vapor cells, and heated-wire sealing methods designed to reduce geometry-induced systematic errors.

Optically pumped magnetometry NMR gyroscopes Rb–Xe vapor cells Spin relaxation COMSOL

Now: Neutral-atom tweezer arrays and QIS

My current research direction is the construction of mixed-species neutral-atom tweezer arrays using Rb and Yb. I am especially interested in the experimental infrastructure that enables scalable control: optical system design, ultra-high-vacuum integration, frequency-stabilized lasers, site-resolved control, and coherent many-body dynamics.

Rb–Yb tweezer arrays Quantum information Quantum simulation Optical systems UHV

Selected research questions

How can controllable neutral-atom arrays be engineered into reliable platforms for computation, simulation, and precision measurement? How can lessons from precision spin dynamics and systematic-error control inform future quantum devices?

Methods and tools

Experimental AMO physics, optical and laser systems, ultra-high-vacuum hardware, vapor-cell fabrication, magnetic-field control, signal processing, Python, Mathematica, COMSOL Multiphysics, LabVIEW, Origin, and SolidWorks.
